About Lumbini

The history of the place where the Buddha was born.

The birth of the Buddha

According to Buddhist tradition, Siddhartha Gautama was born in the Lumbini garden to Queen Maya Devi and King Suddhodana of the Shakya clan, whose capital was Kapilvastu. Maya Devi was travelling to her parents' home when she stopped in the garden and gave birth.

Siddhartha grew up as a prince. He later left his palace to search for the end of suffering, became the Buddha at Bodh Gaya, and taught for the rest of his life. Buddhism grew from his teachings.

Historians do not agree on the exact date of his birth. It is usually placed around the 6th or 5th century BCE.

History timeline

Important moments in the story of Lumbini.

About 6th century BCE – Birth of Siddhartha

Prince Siddhartha is born in the Lumbini garden. Excavations inside the Maya Devi Temple have found traces of very early structures, which archaeologists link to this period.

249 BCE – Emperor Ashoka visits

The Mauryan emperor Ashoka visits Lumbini as a pilgrim and sets up a stone pillar. Its inscription says that the Buddha was born here and that the village was freed from some taxes.

Later centuries – Pilgrims and decline

Chinese monks such as Faxian and Xuanzang described Lumbini in their travel records. Over time the site was abandoned and covered by forest.

1896 – The pillar is rediscovered

Archaeologists identify the Ashoka Pillar again, and Lumbini is confirmed as the Buddha's birthplace.

1978 – Master plan

A master plan by the Japanese architect Kenzo Tange is adopted to develop the sacred area, the monastic zones and the surrounding park.

1997 – UNESCO World Heritage Site

Lumbini is added to the UNESCO World Heritage List for its importance to world Buddhism.

Cultural heritage

Lumbini is a place of living culture as well as ruins.

Buddha Jayanti

Every year on the full moon of Baisakh (April/May) people gather to pray, light lamps and celebrate the Buddha's birth.

Many Buddhist traditions

Monasteries in Lumbini follow Theravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana traditions, showing the diversity of Buddhism.

Art and archaeology

The Lumbini Museum and nearby sites preserve ancient objects, coins and sculptures from the region.
